# Heart Failure Classification and Therapy (Practice Protocol)

Distilled from the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart failure guideline for use in our
primary-care panel. Paraphrased; consult the source for complete
recommendations.

## The four stages (trajectory, not severity snapshot)

- **Stage A — at risk.** No symptoms, no structural disease, but carrying
  risk: hypertension, diabetes, obesity, cardiotoxin exposure, or family
  history of cardiomyopathy. Much of our chronic-care panel is Stage A.
- **Stage B — pre-heart failure.** Still no symptoms, but structural
  disease, elevated filling pressures, or elevated natriuretic peptides /
  persistently elevated troponin.
- **Stage C — symptomatic.** Structural disease with current or prior
  symptoms (dyspnea, fatigue, congestion).
- **Stage D — advanced.** Symptoms interfering with daily life, recurrent
  hospitalizations despite optimized therapy.

Stages only move forward; a Stage C patient who feels well is still Stage C.

## Ejection-fraction classes

| Class | LVEF |
| --- | --- |
| HFrEF (reduced) | 40% or below |
| HFmrEF (mildly reduced) | 41–49% |
| HFpEF (preserved) | 50% or higher |
| HFimpEF (improved) | previously 40% or below, now above 40% |

HFimpEF matters in primary care: **do not deprescribe** guideline-directed
therapy just because the EF recovered — withdrawal risks relapse.

## Prevention in Stage A/B (where primary care wins)

- Blood pressure controlled per our HTN protocol (below 130/80).
- In type 2 diabetes with cardiovascular disease or high risk, an SGLT2
  inhibitor helps prevent heart-failure hospitalization.
- Natriuretic-peptide screening in at-risk patients can prompt early
  echo and intervention.
- In Stage B with EF 40% or below, an ACE inhibitor and a beta-blocker
  are indicated to prevent symptomatic HF.

## GDMT for HFrEF — the four pillars

1. **RAS inhibition** — ARNI preferred (ACEi or ARB when ARNI is not
   feasible).
2. **Evidence-based beta-blocker** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or
   bisoprolol).
3. **Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** (spironolactone or
   eplerenone), with potassium and renal monitoring.
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor**, with or without diabetes.

All four, titrated to target doses as tolerated, as early as tolerated.
Diuretics manage congestion but do not modify disease. For HFmrEF and
HFpEF, an SGLT2 inhibitor is the anchor recommendation, with symptom and
comorbidity management (especially hypertension and atrial fibrillation).

Referral checkpoints: persistent EF of 35% or below despite 3 months of
optimized GDMT prompts an electrophysiology/device discussion; any Stage D
features prompt advanced-HF referral.

## What the Co-Pilot should flag

1. A charted HF diagnosis with fewer than four pillar classes on the active
   medication list (for HFrEF) — name which pillar is absent, citing the
   medication rows and this protocol.
2. Rising weight or blood pressure trend plus an HF diagnosis — possible
   congestion signal worth raising for today's visit.
3. Diabetic or hypertensive patients (Stage A) whose regimen review is due
   — prevention framing, not diagnosis.
4. Missing EF data: if no echo result is in the chart, the EF class is
   unknown — state that as a gap; never infer an EF class from therapy.
