# CKD Screening and Management in Diabetes (Practice Protocol)

Distilled for internal use from the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026,
Section 11 (Chronic Kidney Disease and Risk Management). Paraphrased; see
the source for the full recommendations and evidence grades.

## Who we screen, and how often

- **Every patient with type 2 diabetes: screen at diagnosis, then at least
  annually.** For type 1 diabetes, begin 5 years after diagnosis.
- Screening = **both** a spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) **and**
  an estimated GFR. One without the other is an incomplete screen — the
  Co-Pilot should surface a missing ACR even when a recent eGFR exists,
  because albuminuria is detected years before eGFR falls in many patients.

## What counts as CKD

- Urine ACR of 30 mg/g or higher, **or** eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m²,
  persisting on at least two measurements over three or more months.
- A single abnormal value is a prompt to confirm, not a diagnosis. Repeat an
  abnormal ACR — transient elevations occur with exercise, fever, marked
  hyperglycemia, and uncontrolled hypertension.
- Once CKD is established, monitor ACR and eGFR one to four times per year
  depending on stage (twice yearly is our floor for most).

## Treatment protocol

- **Blood pressure:** target below 130/80 mmHg. For patients with
  hypertension and a urine ACR of 30 mg/g or higher, use an ACE inhibitor or
  ARB titrated to the maximum tolerated dose. Do not combine ACEi and ARB.
- **SGLT2 inhibitor:** recommended for type 2 diabetes with CKD when eGFR is
  20 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher, particularly with ACR of 200 mg/g or higher —
  proven to slow CKD progression and reduce cardiovascular events. Once
  started, it may be continued as eGFR declines until dialysis or transplant.
- **Finerenone** (nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist): add
  for patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD with persistent albuminuria
  despite a maximally tolerated ACEi/ARB, to reduce kidney progression and
  cardiovascular events. Monitor potassium.
- **Glycemia:** optimizing glucose control slows CKD onset and progression;
  the A1c target follows our glycemic protocol (see `ada-soc-glycemic`).
- **Metformin and falling eGFR:** follow the renal dosing protocol
  (`metformin-egfr`) — reassess below 45 and stop below 30.
- **Protein intake:** for CKD not on dialysis, about 0.8 g/kg/day is the
  reference point; avoid high-protein diets.

## When we refer to nephrology

- eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² (planning for kidney replacement therapy).
- Continuously rising ACR or persistent ACR above 300 mg/g, rapid eGFR
  decline, uncertainty about etiology, or refractory hypertension /
  hyperkalemia complicating management.

## What this means for the between-rooms briefing

For a diabetic patient, the Co-Pilot should treat these as surfaceable gaps:

1. No urine ACR result in the last 12 months.
2. No eGFR in the last 12 months.
3. A new eGFR below 60 or a new ACR of 30 mg/g or higher that has not been
   repeated for confirmation.
4. Confirmed CKD without an ACEi/ARB (if hypertensive with albuminuria) or
   without an SGLT2 inhibitor (if eGFR is 20 or higher).
5. An eGFR that has crossed below 45 or 30 while metformin remains active.
