# Blood Pressure Classification and Treatment (Practice Protocol)

Our office protocol for adult blood pressure, distilled from the 2017
ACC/AHA hypertension guideline. Paraphrased for internal use. Note: a 2025
AHA/ACC update exists; this protocol intentionally tracks the 2017
thresholds our decision support and quality measures were validated
against, which the 2025 document carries forward for classification.

## Classification (office readings, average of 2 or more, 2 or more occasions)

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and | below 80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |

The higher of the two components sets the category. Confirm new diagnoses
with out-of-office measurement (home BP or ambulatory monitoring) before
labeling — white-coat and masked hypertension are both common.

## When we start medication

- **Stage 2 (at or above 140/90):** start antihypertensive drug therapy
  alongside lifestyle changes; two agents of different classes are
  reasonable when starting well above goal.
- **Stage 1 (130–139/80–89):** start drug therapy if the patient has
  clinical cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or an
  estimated 10-year ASCVD risk of 10% or higher. Otherwise, lifestyle
  changes first with reassessment in 3 to 6 months.
- **Elevated (120–129/<80):** lifestyle only; reassess in 3 to 6 months.

First-line agents: thiazide-type diuretics, calcium channel blockers, ACE
inhibitors, or ARBs. For CKD with albuminuria, prefer ACEi/ARB (see
`ada-soc-ckd-dm`). Never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.

## Treatment target

**Below 130/80 mmHg for most treated adults**, including those with
diabetes, CKD, stable coronary disease, and adults 65 or older who are
community-dwelling and ambulatory. Reassess within about one month of
starting or changing therapy; once at goal, follow at 3-to-6-month
intervals.

## Lifestyle prescription (every stage, every visit)

Weight loss (expect roughly 1 mmHg per kg lost), DASH-style eating pattern,
dietary sodium reduction toward 1,500 mg/day, potassium-rich foods unless
contraindicated by CKD or potassium-raising drugs, 90–150 minutes of
aerobic exercise weekly, alcohol at most 2 drinks daily for men and 1 for
women, and tobacco cessation.

## What the Co-Pilot should flag

1. Two or more consecutive office readings at 130/80 or higher in a patient
   with no hypertension diagnosis on the problem list — a possible missed
   diagnosis, pending out-of-office confirmation.
2. A treated hypertensive whose latest readings are at or above 130/80 —
   above goal; note how long and how far above, citing the vitals rows.
3. Readings at or above 140/90 on treatment — Stage-2-range despite
   therapy; surface prominently.
4. An upward trend across three or more visits even if each single reading
   seems unremarkable — trajectory is the point of the briefing.

Always cite the specific vitals entries (date and reading) alongside this
protocol; if only one reading exists, say the classification is provisional
by definition.
