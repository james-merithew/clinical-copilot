# Glycemic Targets and Monitoring (Practice Protocol)

This is our clinic's working protocol for glycemic goals in nonpregnant
adults, distilled from the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (Section 6).
It is a paraphrase for internal use; consult the source for full context.

## Diagnostic thresholds

We diagnose diabetes when any of the following is met and confirmed
(repeat the same test, or two abnormal tests from the same sample):

- Hemoglobin A1c of 6.5% or higher
- Fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher
- 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher on a 75-g OGTT
- Random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with classic hyperglycemia symptoms

Prediabetes: A1c 5.7–6.4%, fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL, or 2-hour OGTT
value 140–199 mg/dL. Patients in this band get lifestyle counseling and at
least annual re-testing.

## A1c goals

- **Most nonpregnant adults: A1c below 7%.** This is the default target for
  our panel and the number the Co-Pilot should treat as "at goal."
- **A less stringent goal (below 8%)** is appropriate for patients with
  limited life expectancy, long-standing disease with established vascular
  complications, extensive comorbidity, or a history of severe hypoglycemia.
- A tighter goal (below 6.5%) may be reasonable for selected patients early
  in the disease course when it can be achieved without significant
  hypoglycemia or polypharmacy burden.

Individualization is the rule: age, frailty, hypoglycemia risk, and patient
preference all move the target. Document the chosen target in the chart.

## Glucose targets when using meter or CGM data

- Preprandial capillary glucose: 80–130 mg/dL
- Peak postprandial glucose: below 180 mg/dL
- For CGM users: time in range (70–180 mg/dL) above 70%, time below
  70 mg/dL under 4%, and time below 54 mg/dL under 1%. The glucose
  management indicator (GMI) supplements, but does not replace, lab A1c.

## Monitoring cadence

- **At goal and stable therapy: A1c at least twice a year.**
- **Not at goal, or therapy recently changed: A1c quarterly** (every
  3 months) until stable.
- Point-of-care A1c at the visit is acceptable for treatment decisions.

For our workflow this means: an A1c above 7% that is more than three months
old with no interval action is a care gap worth surfacing; an A1c above
target that is rising across two or more consecutive results should be
flagged for therapy intensification discussion at today's visit.

## Hypoglycemia

Ask about hypoglycemia at every visit for patients on insulin or
sulfonylureas. Level 1 is glucose below 70 mg/dL; level 2 is below
54 mg/dL; level 3 is any event requiring assistance. Any level 2 or 3
event triggers a regimen review and re-evaluation of the A1c target —
a "great" A1c achieved through recurrent lows is a failure, not a success.
Prescribe glucagon for anyone at elevated risk of level 2/3 hypoglycemia.

## How the Co-Pilot should use this document

When citing this protocol: the default comparison line for adult A1c is 7%,
the relaxed line is 8%, and trend direction matters as much as the latest
value. Never state a patient-specific target as fact unless it is documented
in the chart; otherwise present the population default with this citation.
