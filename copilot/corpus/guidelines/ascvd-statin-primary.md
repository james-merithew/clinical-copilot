# Statin Therapy for Primary Prevention (Practice Protocol)

Our office protocol for deciding who gets a statin before any
cardiovascular event, distilled from the 2018 AHA/ACC multisociety
cholesterol guideline (cross-referenced with the USPSTF 2022 statin
recommendation). Paraphrased; the source documents govern.

## The four primary-prevention groups

1. **LDL-C 190 mg/dL or higher (any age 20–75):** start a high-intensity
   statin. No risk calculation needed — this level suggests severe
   hypercholesterolemia, often familial.
2. **Diabetes, age 40–75:** start at least a moderate-intensity statin
   regardless of the calculated 10-year risk. Escalate to high-intensity
   when multiple diabetes-specific risk enhancers are present (long
   duration, albuminuria, eGFR below 60, retinopathy, neuropathy,
   ABI below 0.9).
3. **Age 40–75, LDL-C 70–189 mg/dL, no diabetes:** decision is driven by
   the 10-year ASCVD risk estimate (pooled cohort equations) after a
   clinician–patient risk discussion:
   - **Below 5% (low):** lifestyle; no statin.
   - **5 to 7.4% (borderline):** consider a moderate-intensity statin if
     risk enhancers are present.
   - **7.5 to 19.9% (intermediate):** start a moderate-intensity statin
     when enhancers and the discussion favor treatment; aim for an LDL-C
     reduction of 30–49%.
   - **20% or higher (high):** high-intensity statin aiming for an LDL-C
     reduction of 50% or more.
4. **Uncertain intermediate cases:** a coronary artery calcium score can
   arbitrate — CAC 0 supports deferring (unless the patient smokes, has
   diabetes, or a family history of premature ASCVD); CAC 100 or higher
   (or 75th percentile or above) favors starting.

Risk enhancers include: family history of premature ASCVD, persistent
LDL-C of 160 or higher, CKD, metabolic syndrome, inflammatory disease,
premature menopause or preeclampsia history, South Asian ancestry,
persistent triglycerides of 175 or higher.

## Intensity reference

- High-intensity (LDL-C drop of 50% or more): atorvastatin 40–80 mg,
  rosuvastatin 20–40 mg.
- Moderate-intensity (30–49%): atorvastatin 10–20 mg, rosuvastatin
  5–10 mg, simvastatin 20–40 mg, pravastatin 40–80 mg.

## Monitoring

Fasting lipids 4 to 12 weeks after starting or changing dose to confirm
the expected percentage reduction and adherence, then every 3 to 12
months as clinically indicated. Statin-associated muscle symptoms get a
structured rechallenge, not reflexive abandonment.

## What the Co-Pilot should flag

1. A diabetic patient aged 40–75 with no active statin on the medication
   list — a direct protocol gap; cite the medication table and this
   document.
2. Any LDL-C result of 190 mg/dL or higher without a high-intensity
   statin active.
3. A rising LDL trend in a patient already on a statin — adherence or
   intensity question for today's visit.
4. When the 10-year risk score is not in the chart, the Co-Pilot must not
   compute or guess it — name it as the missing input for group-3
   decisions.
