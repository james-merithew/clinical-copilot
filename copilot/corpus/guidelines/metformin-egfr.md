# Metformin and Kidney Function (Practice Protocol)

Our renal-dosing protocol for metformin, distilled from the FDA's revised
labeling guidance (which moved the standard from serum creatinine cutoffs
to eGFR). Paraphrased for internal use. Rationale: metformin is renally
cleared, and accumulation in significant renal impairment raises the risk
of lactic acidosis — rare but serious.

## The eGFR bands (mL/min/1.73 m²)

| eGFR | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 60 or higher | Full dosing. Check eGFR at least annually. |
| 45 to 59 | Continue; standard dosing generally acceptable. Recheck eGFR more often (every 3–6 months in our practice) if trending down. |
| 30 to 44 | **Do not start** metformin in this band. If the patient is already on it and eGFR falls here, reassess benefit and risk; if continued, our practice halves exposure — maximum 1,000 mg per day — with eGFR every 3 months. |
| Below 30 | **Contraindicated. Stop metformin.** |

## Practical rules we follow

- **Initiation:** eGFR before the first prescription. Do not initiate below
  45. Between 45 and 60, initiation is acceptable with closer monitoring.
- **Monitoring:** eGFR at least annually for everyone on metformin; more
  frequently (every 3–6 months) when eGFR is below 60, the trend is
  downward, or an interacting condition (CHF, dehydration risk,
  nephrotoxic co-medication) is present.
- **Iodinated contrast:** hold metformin at the time of (or before) the
  procedure when eGFR is 30–60, with liver disease/alcoholism/heart
  failure, or with intra-arterial contrast. Recheck eGFR 48 hours after;
  resume only if renal function is stable.
- **Sick days:** advise patients to pause metformin during acute illness
  with vomiting, diarrhea, or poor intake — acute kidney injury converts a
  safe dose into an unsafe one.
- **B12:** long-term metformin can lower vitamin B12; periodic B12
  measurement is reasonable, especially with anemia or neuropathy.

## Why this document exists for the Co-Pilot

Metformin's chart data and lab data live in different tables and are
updated by different people at different times — exactly the divergence
the briefing must catch. The protocol turns into three deterministic
checks:

1. **Active metformin + latest eGFR below 30** — contraindication;
   surface as a can't-miss item citing the prescription row, the lab row,
   and this protocol.
2. **Active metformin + latest eGFR 30–44** — dose-reassessment flag;
   note whether the current dose exceeds 1,000 mg/day.
3. **Active metformin + eGFR trending down toward 45** (for example,
   consecutive values crossing from the 60s into the 50s) — not an alarm,
   but worth one line in the briefing so the trajectory is on the
   physician's radar before it becomes rule 1 or 2.

If no eGFR exists within the last 12 months for a patient on metformin,
that absence is itself the finding — report the monitoring gap, never a
presumed value.
